# Recruiter Communication Style Guide

This document defines the tone, structure, and approach for responding to recruiter outreach.

## Core Principles

1. **Direct and efficient** - No fluff, get to the point
2. **Facts over spin** - State reality clearly, no corporate speak
3. **Enable self-filtering** - Give recruiters enough info to decide if it's worth both our time
4. **Respect but don't pander** - Professional without being deferential
5. **No em dashes** - Keep punctuation simple

## Tone and Voice

### Do:
- Be casual and conversational ("Thanks for reaching out")
- Use short sentences and paragraphs
- State facts plainly ("I'm not actively looking")
- Be direct about dealbreakers ("If you're working with traditional VP/CTO roles, that's not my fit")
- Keep it friendly but not overly warm

### Don't:
- Use corporate buzzwords ("synergy", "leverage", "circle back")
- Write long paragraphs
- Apologize or explain excessively
- Use exclamation marks everywhere
- Show off or humble-brag
- Use em dashes (—)

## Structure

### Initial Response Template

```
Hey [Name],

[Acknowledge their message]

[Current status + timeline]

[Brief context about current role and approach]

[Clarify what you're NOT looking for]

[Clarify what you ARE looking for]

[Other key criteria in bullet or sentence form]

[Clear call to action]

Cheers,
Serban
```

### Length Guidelines
- **Initial response:** 150-200 words max
- **Follow-up:** Even shorter, 50-100 words
- If they need more info, they'll ask

## Key Messages to Communicate

### Always Include:
1. **Timeline** - "Realistically wouldn't start anything before mid-2026"
2. **Player-coach requirement** - Hands-on technical + leadership
3. **What's NOT a fit** - Traditional management roles
4. **Key criteria** - Remote-first, compensation range, type of work

### Never Include (unless specifically relevant):
- Full work history (they can check LinkedIn/portfolio)
- Detailed explanations of current projects
- Links to CV or portfolio (unless discussing specific opportunity)
- Apologies for constraints or preferences

## Example Phrases

### Good:
- "Profile is correct but I'm not actively looking"
- "That's deliberate, not changing"
- "If something specific matches this, happy to chat"
- "I'm a player-coach (hands-on as architect/engineer + leading teams)"
- "Otherwise feel free to reach out if something comes up later"

### Bad:
- "I'm currently exploring opportunities that align with my career trajectory"
- "I'm passionate about leveraging my technical expertise"
- "I'd love to jump on a quick call to discuss my background"
- "Unfortunately, I don't think this is the right fit at this time"
- "I'm excited about the possibility of..."

## Handling Different Scenarios

### Generic Outreach (No Specific Role)
- Acknowledge but set clear boundaries
- Provide filtering criteria
- Skip the discovery call unless they have something specific
- Keep door open for future with concrete opportunities

### Specific Role (Good Potential Fit)
- Express interest
- Ask clarifying questions (team size, technical involvement, timeline)
- Be willing to have a conversation
- Still set expectations about timeline and requirements

### Specific Role (Clear Mismatch)
- Thank them but decline quickly
- Briefly explain why it's not a fit
- Don't waste their time or yours
- Keep it friendly

### Follow-up from Previous Conversation
- Be even more direct
- Reference previous context
- Give yes/no answer quickly

## Decision Tree

```
Is it a specific opportunity?
├─ No → Generic response with filtering criteria
└─ Yes
    └─ Does it match player-coach requirement?
        ├─ No → Polite decline
        └─ Yes
            └─ Does it match timeline (mid-2026+)?
                ├─ No → Mention timeline mismatch, but express interest for future
                └─ Yes → Express interest, schedule conversation
```

## Cultural Context

**Transylvanian approach** - Don't show off, but state facts clearly:
- ❌ "I've had tremendous success scaling organizations"
- ✅ "I scaled the org from 6 to 40 people while going from 3 to 11 products"

**Builder identity** - Technical work is not something to grow out of:
- ❌ "I'm looking to transition into more strategic roles"
- ✅ "I'm hands-on as PM/architect/engineer. That's deliberate, not changing"

## Red Lines

Never compromise on these in communication:
1. Timeline (mid-2026+)
2. Player-coach requirement (hands-on technical + leadership)
3. No pure management roles
4. Respect for current commitments

## Quality Over Quantity

Remember: If a recruiter or company filters you out based on being direct and clear about requirements, that's working as intended. The goal is quality conversations with the right opportunities, not maximum response rate.
