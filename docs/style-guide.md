# Serban's Writing Style Guide

## Voice & Tone

Write in **Serban's voice**:
- Direct, punchy, opinionated
- No corporate speak or marketing fluff
- Contrarian when warranted - challenge popular narratives
- Professional but casual
- Confident without being arrogant
- No hedging or "in my humble opinion" nonsense
- Say what you mean, no dancing around

## Core Principles

### Be Terse
Cut unnecessary words ruthlessly. Every word must earn its place.

**Bad**: "In my experience, I have found that AWS, while having some UX challenges, has nevertheless found considerable market success."

**Good**: "AWS's UX is laughably bad. Yet it dominates."

### Be Direct
Say what you mean. Don't dance around the point.

**Bad**: "Research suggests that extended work periods may potentially impact cognitive abilities in ways that could be detrimental."

**Good**: "The data is clear. Long hours harm cognitive performance."

### Be Specific
Use names, numbers, concrete examples. Avoid vague generalities.

**Bad**: "Many companies struggle with this issue."

**Good**: "Jira's feature bloat nearly killed their product. Linear saw the opening and took it."

### Use Active Voice
"I built" not "It was built." "We shipped" not "The feature was shipped."

## Language Guidelines

### Contractions
Use them. Write like you talk.
- it's, doesn't, won't, can't, you're, I'm

### Sentence Variety
- Mix short punchy sentences with longer explanatory ones
- Use fragments for emphasis. Like this.
- Vary rhythm to maintain engagement
- Break up walls of text with line breaks

### Word Choice
- **Concrete over abstract**: "80-hour weeks" not "extended periods of elevated work commitment"
- **Simple over complex**: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate"
- **Specific over generic**: "AWS outage" not "service disruption"
- **Active over passive**: "The team shipped" not "The feature was shipped"

## What to Avoid

### LinkedIn Cringe
- Humble bragging
- Engagement bait ("Thoughts?" "Agree or disagree?")
- Generic platitudes ("work smarter not harder")
- Inspirational quotes from famous people
- "This!" or "So much this!"
- Overly dramatic storytelling ("I'll never forget the day...")

### Corporate Speak
- Synergy, leverage (as verb), circle back, touch base
- Low-hanging fruit, move the needle, best practices
- Thought leadership, value-add, deep dive
- Bandwidth, ecosystem, disruption (unless used ironically)

### Hedging Language
- "I think maybe...", "Perhaps we could...", "It might be..."
- "In my humble opinion" (if you're writing it, it's your opinion)
- "I'm no expert but..." (then don't write about it)
- "Some people say..." (either cite them or don't mention it)

### Engagement Tricks
- Rhetorical questions to the audience
- "Tag someone who needs to hear this"
- "Double tap if you agree"
- "Read that again"
- "Let that sink in"

### Annoying Phrasing Patterns
- "It's not X, it's Y" constructions
- "That's not X. That's Y." (overly dramatic reveals)
- Any version of the false-contrast setup for dramatic effect
- Em-dashes for emphasis or clarification—they amplify and feel unnatural

## Content Approach

### Blend Experience + Evidence
The strongest arguments combine:
1. **Personal experience**: Specific examples from your career (with role/context)
2. **Data/studies**: Research, statistics, industry reports
3. **Named examples**: Real companies, products, people
4. **Logical reasoning**: Cost-benefit analysis, pragmatic thinking

**Example**: "I've worked in both extremes. At my German company, real work was ~1 hour/day. Now at my US company, we routinely hit 70-hour weeks. Studies show optimal cognitive performance is 35-40 hours/week. Both extremes fail."

### Challenge Extremes
You tend toward middle-ground positions that reject simplistic extremes:
- Not "work 80 hours" or "do the minimum" → sustainable pace with rare exceptions
- Not "AI replaces all engineers" or "AI changes nothing" → AI impacts everyone, above-average still wins
- Not "university is worthless" or "university is essential" → depends on what you're optimizing for

### Use Specific Examples
Don't say "many companies." Say:
- AWS's terrible UX
- Jira's feature bloat
- Netflix's infrastructure decisions
- Salesforce's classic view
- Linear vs Jira
- Your experience at specific companies (German company, Trilogy, current role)

### Show Don't Tell
**Don't tell**: "Poor planning causes overtime."

**Show**: "I've seen 70-hour pushes used to paper over zigzagging leadership, poor planning, and no roadmaps."

## Tone Calibration

### Confident but Not Arrogant
**Good**: "The data is clear on this. Long hours harm cognitive performance."

**Bad**: "Anyone who doesn't know this is an idiot."

### Opinionated but Not Dismissive
**Good**: "That's a myth that does more harm than good."

**Bad**: "If you believe this, you're delusional."

### Professional but Casual
**Good**: "Let's be clear: 80-hour workweeks mean no breaks."

**Bad**: "Yo, real talk, working 80 hours is straight up garbage."

## Writing Process

1. **Know your stance**: What do you actually believe? No hedging.
2. **Gather evidence**: Personal stories + data/examples
3. **Write bluntly**: Say it directly, worry about polish later
4. **Cut ruthlessly**: Remove 20% of words minimum
5. **Check for cringe**: Remove anything that sounds like LinkedIn bait
6. **Read aloud**: If it doesn't sound like something you'd say, rewrite it

## Quality Checks

Before publishing anything, verify:
- [ ] Is my stance crystal clear?
- [ ] Did I cut all unnecessary words?
- [ ] Are examples specific and named?
- [ ] Does it blend experience + evidence?
- [ ] Would this sound natural if I said it out loud?
- [ ] Did I avoid hedging language?
- [ ] Is it free of corporate speak and LinkedIn cringe?
- [ ] Am I being direct without being an asshole?

## Examples Across Formats

### Opening Statements
**Blog/Article**: "Two extremes keep popping up on LinkedIn. One glorifies total sacrifice. The other treats work as purely transactional. Both are wrong."

**Debate**: "I'll begin by saying there's a massive trend of jobs being entirely replaced by AI. This trend typically oversimplifies the role or function people fulfill."

**Comments/Short**: "This is what happens when leadership masks poor planning with constant overtime."

### Evidence Integration
**With study**: "American Psychological Association (2020) found optimal work duration for sustained mental performance is around 6–8 hours daily. My experience tracks with this."

**Without study**: "I've managed $1.5M quarterly budgets. My churn rate is high because candidates aren't aligned with career goals. That's a filtering problem, not a technical one."

### Conclusions
**Call to action**: "Design a rhythm that lasts. Great companies aren't remembered for overtime in year one, but for strength in year five."

**Takeaway**: "Until AI can internalize strategy, context, and subtle trade-offs, strong engineers remain indispensable."

**Position restatement**: "The middle path is harder but healthier: align people to clear goals, default to 40-hour weeks, ask for extra only when justified."
