About

I’m Serban. I run Product Engineering for WS.Eng, Trilogy’s new-products department: around 40 people, about ten products in parallel and a $15M yearly budget.

I took over in 2023 with six backend engineers, the survivors of a team that had shipped almost nothing in a quarter. I merged in the frontend team, rebuilt engineering to around 30 people and later took on product as well. Engineering is about the same size it was in my first year, and it ships 10x as much.

Most of that jump came from the machinery we built around AI. In 2026 I started Nex, our in-house AI software factory, and built about 90% of it, mostly by having Nex build itself. It now writes nearly all of our pull requests, while people own the plans, the decisions and the evidence.

My bias: AI makes average work cheap, so strong engineering judgment matters more than ever. A few strong engineers steering agents outship a large team of average ones. I write about how we run engineering this way at wseng.ai.

Before Trilogy, I spent six years at msg systems going from junior to lead engineer (SAP Developer Hero). I also wrote cloud architecture specs at DevFactory, co-founded a startup, and taught Software Design at TU Cluj-Napoca.

Nex

2026 to present

Creator and architect. Built about 90% of it.

Writes nearly all of our pull requests. Around 1,000 merged a week across the department, one production release a day, and about 80 people using it.

An AI software factory that takes a ticket or a chat message all the way to production. It scopes the work, writes a plan, implements it, tests it on a live environment built for that one pull request, and drives it to merge.

We rent the model and swap it whenever a better one ships. The harness is ours: an isolated cloud sandbox per task, organizational memory with provenance, independent AI review, and guardrails tested like production code. I built most of it by having Nex build itself.

StudyFilm

2025 to present

Architect, hands-on engineer

Records millions of study sessions a quarter for thousands of Alpha School students. Vision models turn them into coaching for students, parents and guides.

Three pieces: a desktop recorder, a vision pipeline, and a web app for parents and guides. The desktop app records what students do while they learn, plus webcam and microphone during tests.

Vision LLMs analyze short windows of each session to find wasted time, write coaching tips and flag possible cheating. Every change is measured against a human-annotated data set. One input change cut false positives on our hardest cases by 27 points. I presented the lessons at Compass AI 2026.
